# Event evidence policy

## Source priority

1. Exchange/company filings and regulator or government policy documents.
2. Company investor-relations releases and official transcripts.
3. Named, attributable reporting from established financial media.
4. Broker research when assumptions and publication time are visible.
5. Social posts and unattributed summaries only as hypotheses to verify.

Search by security code, company name, event date, and suspected mechanism. Prefer the original
document over a story quoting it. Record publication time and whether it preceded the price
move. Do not repeat a report's causal headline as fact.

## Attribution test

A candidate driver is stronger when it satisfies all four conditions:

- timing: public before the move or during the session;
- novelty: changed information rather than a known fact;
- mechanism: plausibly changes cash flow, discount rate, risk, or positioning;
- relative evidence: the security moved differently from its benchmark or sector.

Conflicting evidence must remain visible. Rank drivers as primary, contributing, or unproven.
